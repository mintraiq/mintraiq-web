# The monthly food-price pitch

`PRIORITIES.md` G #64. Reusable: Stats NZ publishes food prices **monthly,
mid-month, for the prior month**, inside the *Selected price indexes* release
(the Food Price Index stopped being its own release after September 2023).
Every NZ newsroom covers it every month. That is a standing slot with a
guaranteed news hook and a journalist who needs a different angle each time.

**This costs emails and reaches more New Zealanders than a year of organic
social.** It is the highest-leverage distribution available at NZ scale.

---

## The one rule that makes this honest

**Mintr has 8 production users. Nothing in any pitch may imply a dataset we do
not have.** No "our users' data shows", no "we're seeing", no aggregate claim
about real baskets. There is no such dataset, and a sample of 8 could not
support one if there were.

The angle does not need it. **The whole story is in the public release** — we
are useful because we work with that release every month and can explain what
it hides, not because we hold private data.

The `marketing/pillars.md` verb rule applies to a pitch and to a live interview
exactly as to a post: **see, spot, notice, know, track** — never switch,
refinance, invest, optimise. A pitch is a representation like any other.

---

## The angle: the headline number describes almost nobody

Every outlet runs the same sentence — "food prices rose X%". That average is the
one figure that applies to no actual household, because the categories under it
move in opposite directions.

**Worked example, August 2026 release** (Stats NZ, *Selected price indexes:
August 2026*, published 18 September 2026 — figures as recorded in
`marketing/reels/2026-09-20-food-index/claims.md`):

| On-screen | Annual change |
|---|---|
| Food, all groups — **the headline** | **+1.9%** |
| Meat, poultry and fish | **+3.4%** |
| Restaurant meals | **+3.2%** |
| Fruit and vegetables | **−1.6%** |

A five-point spread inside a 1.9% headline. A household that eats a lot of meat
and a household that eats a lot of vegetables had opposite years, and the number
in the headline belongs to neither.

**These figures are the August example. Re-derive every number from the new
release before sending, and re-source it in a fresh `claims.md`.** Never carry a
figure over from a previous month — that is the exact defect the claims gate
exists to stop.

## Email 1 — the standing offer. Send BEFORE a release, once.

A cold email on release morning is noise; they are already writing. Land first,
then be available.

> **Subject:** Local source on the monthly food price numbers
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I saw your piece on [last month's release]. I build a NZ budgeting app and I
> work through the Selected price indexes food tables every month — I pull the
> weighted average prices into a tool that charts them against what people
> actually paid on their receipts.
>
> If it's ever useful on release day, I can turn the category spread around
> quickly — which groups moved against the headline, and what that means for a
> household whose shopping doesn't look like the national average. Happy to be a
> quick phone call or a couple of lines by email, whichever suits, and happy to
> be ignored if it isn't useful.
>
> [Name], founder, Mintr — [phone]

Short, no attachment, no pitch deck, no product paragraph. The product mention is
one clause and exists only to establish why you'd know.

## Email 2 — release day. Send within 60–90 minutes of the tables going live.

> **Subject:** Food prices — the spread under today's [X]% figure
>
> Hi [name],
>
> Today's number is [X]% for the year. The spread under it is wider than the
> headline suggests:
>
> - [Category] [+X.X%]
> - [Category] [+X.X%]
> - [Category] [−X.X%]
>
> So [plain-English consequence — e.g. a household that buys a lot of [category]
> had a materially different year from one that doesn't].
>
> All from today's release, table [N] — happy to walk through it if useful.
>
> [Name] — [phone]

**Every figure traceable to the release you are quoting. No rounding in a
flattering direction. No figure from any other month.**

## Who to send to

Do not guess at names. **Open last month's food-price story on each outlet and
take the byline** — that is the person who will be assigned it again.

- RNZ — business desk
- Stuff — business / money
- NZ Herald — business, and the Money vertical
- 1News and ThreeNews — business reporters
- BusinessDesk — more finance-literate, will want the table not the summary
- The Spinoff — different framing; the "what the average hides" angle suits them

Six emails. Send individually, never as a list.

---

## The quote to have ready

Journalists need something a table cannot say. Short, concrete, no jargon:

> "The national average is a real number, but it isn't anyone's shopping. If you
> buy a lot of meat you had a very different year from someone who buys a lot of
> vegetables — and the headline figure is the one number that describes neither
> of them."

**What must not be said**, because it is either unsubstantiated or regulated
advice: any claim about what our users are seeing; any prediction of where prices
go next; anything that tells a listener what to do about it. Explaining what
happened is safe ground and it is enough.

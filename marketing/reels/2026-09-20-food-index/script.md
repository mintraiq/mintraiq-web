# Script — 2026-09-20-food-index

Pillar `number`, format `feed`, 4-slide carousel. No video this week, so there
is no `prompts.md` — nothing here needs Google Flow.

**The beat:** For New Zealanders who feel groceries getting dearer but can't say
where, Mintr charts their own items against the Stats NZ food price index, so
they can see which way their basket actually moved.

The carousel keeps the reel beat sheet, compressed. Slide 1 carries the whole
post, because in the feed it is the only slide anyone sees.

| Slide | Beat | On-screen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | `Food prices rose 1.9%` · the sourced headline, with its month |
| 2 | Ache | `The average hid this` · the four-row spread, the fall in red |
| 3 | Reveal | `An average isn't a basket` · why the headline number isn't yours |
| 4 | Close | `See what your own basket did` · one line, one action, attribution |

## On-screen text

Kept to 3–6 words a line on the display sizes. Most of this is read while
scrolling, and a carousel slide gets about a second before the thumb decides.

1. **Food prices rose 1.9%** — mint on the figure alone
2. **The average hid this** — mint on "hid this"; the −1.6% is the only red
3. **An average isn't a basket** — mint on "isn't a basket"
4. **See what your own basket did** — headline neutral, mint reserved for the CTA

One mint per slide. On slide 2 the rises stay white so the single fall leads the
eye; colouring all four made four things shout and nothing lead.

## Register

No voiceover — this is a static carousel. The writing is flat and factual on the
data slides and only loosens on slide 3, where the point is made. A price post
that sounds excited about food inflation reads badly, and the house style for
this product is calm and documentary rather than energetic.

## What this post does not do

It reports someone else's published statistic and offers to show you your own
figures. It does not tell anyone where to shop, what to buy, or what to do about
a price — that verb belongs to a licensed adviser, not to us.
